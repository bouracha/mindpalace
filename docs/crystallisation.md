# Crystallisation

Some of what happens in a working day is worth more than the day: a principle stated in passing, a mistake you would rather not make twice, an explanation that finally landed. Crystallisation turns that moment into a **crystal**, a short, named, reusable claim, kept in a crystal library and indexed so that it can be found when the same shape of problem comes round again.

This is a pattern, not a rule. Use it as described, change the format, or skip it.

Societies already have crystals: slogans. A good slogan is short and portable, carries a lot of diffuse meaning, and is backed by the thinking that gave rise to it. A crystal is the same thing with its backing kept beside it: the reasoning and where it came from.

## What a crystal is (the format I use)

- **The title is the claim.** "Give the model latitude, fix the mechanism", not "Notes on prompting". A reader gets the lesson from the title alone.
- **One claim per crystal.** A second idea gets a second crystal.
- **A type:** WHAT (how the world is), WHY (a reason or a value) or HOW (a method). Some are two at once.
- **An originator:** whose idea it was, as a [provenance tag](provenance.md), if you use them.
- **A short body:** the crystal in a few sentences; why it matters; where it applies beyond the case that produced it.

Three examples about MindPalace itself are in the template: [mindpalace/crystal-library/](../mindpalace/crystal-library/README.md). They are lessons from my own palace, there to show the format; keep, change or delete them.

## Where crystals come from

The trigger is recognition, not a schedule: a lesson that generalises. You can ask for one in the moment ("crystallise that"), and the [update](update.md) can propose candidates from the sessions it reads. A test that has worked for me before adding one:

- **portable:** it would apply in a different area from the one that produced it;
- **new:** not already in the library;
- **stated as a claim:** something a reader could act on or disagree with.

A new instance of an existing crystal can extend that crystal instead of creating a near-duplicate. Turning candidates down is part of keeping a library useful; one that keeps everything stops being an index.

## Why crystals rather than notes

Notes record what happened. A crystal records what to do the next time. Because each is a single claim with a precise title, the library's index can stay at one line per crystal: the model can scan it cheaply and open only the crystal it needs. Over time the library becomes a shared vocabulary. "That is a superseded-version problem" means the same thing to you and to the model.

## Provenance tags

A crystal can record whose idea it was: `[A]` the author (the human), `[J]` the agent (unvetted), `[J→A]` adopted, `[A+J]` joint, `[J?]` raised and left unanswered. Without a tag, a suggestion from the agent, read back a month later, looks like your own conclusion. The letters are mine; use any you like. More in [provenance.md](provenance.md).

## Crystals and evolution crystals

They answer different questions. A crystal is a portable lesson, kept once, in the library. An [evolution crystal](evolution-crystals.md) is the history of one area: what changed there, and why. Writing an evolution crystal often surfaces a crystal; the reverse is rare.

## Starting your library

The template includes `mindpalace/crystal-library/` with an index and three example crystals. If you keep it, add a row to the index for each new crystal: the title as a link, the type, the tag and one sentence. Rename it, restructure it or start your own; the model follows whatever index you keep.
